    How to use WildScope

    WildScope turns free satellite and map data into simple 0–100 scores for every small square of land, anywhere in the world: where wildlife can live, where nature is richest, and when to go if you hunt. The validated pilot region is Freiburg and the western Black Forest (Germany); any other place is analysed live with ◎ Analyse this area.

    1. Pick a mode at the top of the left panel: Habitat (one species), Biodiversity (variety and naturalness of the landscape) or Hunting (habitat plus live wind, light and your own hunting ground).
    2. Read the colours. Colours show each square's rank in the area on screen: blue = among the lowest, red = among the highest. Click a square for its actual score (0–100) and a plain class: poor, average, good or very good.
    3. Build your own score. Each indicator has a tick box and a weight slider. Untick one, or change its weight, and the map recalculates instantly.
    4. Click any square to see its score and why: every indicator's value, its weight and the points it adds, plus the real measurements behind it (tree cover, NDVI, distance to the nearest road, and so on).
    5. Check the evidence in Insights (right): the score distribution and how well the score matches real deer or wild boar sightings (GBIF), compared fairly with where people report other mammals. Test each indicator on its own shows which indicators the animals really follow; in an analysed area with 40 or more sightings you can tune the weights to them, and WildScope keeps the result only if it beats the pilot weights on held-out ground. Best 30% / Best 10% keeps only the top squares.
    6. Export the score you built as a GeoTIFF and open it in QGIS.
    7. Search a place (top left of the map) or press ✈ Fly to pilot region. Labels adds place names and roads.
    8. Anywhere in the world: pick one from 🌍 Try a place, or search or zoom to any town or valley and press ◎ Analyse this area. WildScope finds the clearest recent Sentinel-2 image, downloads land cover, roads, water and sightings for up to 12 × 12 km, and scores it at 50 m detail in about a minute. The result stays in your browser, and ⊕ Copy link gives a link that opens the same analysis for anyone.
    9. Satellite & reference layers (bottom of the left panel) add the raw data: Sentinel-2 by date, cloud mask, NDVI, DLR crop and forest maps, protected areas.

    The three modes

    Habitat
    Is this a good place for deer or wild boar? Woodland, open feeding land, food, field–forest mosaic, distance from towns and roads, water and moisture, from Sentinel-2, ESA WorldCover and OpenStreetMap. The roe deer weights are fitted to real sightings and tested on areas the fit never saw.
    Biodiversity
    How varied and natural is the landscape? Habitat variety, natural cover, spectral diversity of the vegetation, distance from towns and roads, and water. A proxy for biodiversity potential, not a species count.
    Hunting
    The habitat score plus live wind and scent direction, sunrise, sunset, moon, and your private hunting-ground boundary.

    Scores are relative indices, not probabilities of seeing an animal. Satellites cannot see the animals themselves. Good habitat is not permission to hunt: always follow local law. No 3D globe? Your browser has WebGL (graphics acceleration) switched off; WildScope then opens a simple 2D map with the same scores.
